# Customer Segmentation using RFM Analysis

## Overview

RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) Analysis is a customer segmentation technique widely used in Retail Analytics to identify and classify customers based on purchasing behavior.

The methodology helps businesses understand:

- Which customers generate the highest value
- Which customers are most loyal
- Which customers are at risk of churn
- Which customers should be targeted for marketing campaigns

By analyzing customer transaction history, organizations can improve retention strategies, increase customer lifetime value, and optimize marketing investments.

---

## Business Objective

The primary objective of RFM Analysis is to:

- Identify high-value customers
- Improve customer retention
- Increase repeat purchases
- Personalize marketing campaigns
- Detect potential churn risks
- Improve customer lifetime value (CLV)

---

## RFM Components

### Recency (R)

Measures how recently a customer made a purchase.

Business Interpretation:

- Lower Recency = More Active Customer
- Higher Recency = Less Active Customer

Example:

| Customer | Last Purchase (Days Ago) |
|----------|--------------------------|
| Customer A | 5 |
| Customer B | 120 |

Customer A is considered more engaged.

---

### Frequency (F)

Measures how often a customer makes purchases.

Business Interpretation:

- Higher Frequency = Loyal Customer
- Lower Frequency = Less Engaged Customer

Example:

| Customer | Number of Purchases |
|----------|-------------------|
| Customer A | 15 |
| Customer B | 2 |

Customer A demonstrates stronger loyalty.

---

### Monetary (M)

Measures the total amount spent by the customer.

Business Interpretation:

- Higher Monetary Value = More Valuable Customer
- Lower Monetary Value = Lower Revenue Contribution

Example:

| Customer | Total Spend (₹) |
|----------|---------------|
| Customer A | 50,000 |
| Customer B | 3,000 |

Customer A generates higher business value.

---

## RFM Scoring Methodology

Each customer is assigned scores for:

- Recency
- Frequency
- Monetary

Typical scoring uses a 1–5 scale.

Example:

| Customer | R | F | M |
|------------|---|---|---|
| Customer A | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| Customer B | 2 | 1 | 2 |

These scores are combined to create customer segments.

## Customer Segments

### Champions

Customers who purchased recently, buy frequently, and spend significantly.

Characteristics:

- Highest business value
- Most loyal customers
- Strongest engagement

Recommended Action:

- Reward programs
- VIP offers
- Premium services

---

### Loyal Customers

Customers who make regular purchases and demonstrate consistent engagement.

Recommended Action:

- Cross-selling
- Upselling
- Loyalty programs

---

### Potential Loyalists

Customers showing positive engagement trends who may become loyal customers.

Recommended Action:

- Personalized promotions
- Engagement campaigns

---

### Recent Customers

Customers who have purchased recently but have limited purchase history.

Recommended Action:

- Onboarding campaigns
- Welcome offers

---

### At-Risk Customers

Customers who previously purchased frequently but have become inactive.

Recommended Action:

- Retention campaigns
- Re-engagement offers
- Personalized communication

---

### Lost Customers

Customers showing little or no recent engagement and a low probability of returning.

Recommended Action:

- Win-back campaigns
- Re-activation strategies
